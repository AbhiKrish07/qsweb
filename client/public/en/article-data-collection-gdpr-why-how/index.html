Collecting data: why do it, and above all, how to do it right?

You know it better than anyone: competition is fierce, and consumer expectations are constantly changing. Understanding your customers has therefore become a strategic imperative. And that…

April 17, 2025

  2. 02How to collect and use data in compliance with the GDPR
  3. 03Conclusion: data, a lever to handle with care
  4. 04Frequently asked questions

You know it better than anyone: competition is fierce, and consumer expectations are constantly changing. Understanding your customers has therefore become a strategic imperative. And that involves, among other things, data collection.

But beyond simply collecting it, using data ethically and legally isn’t just recommended: it’s mandatory. That is what this article is about: explaining why collecting data is useful, and above all how to do it with respect for your customers and the law, starting with the GDPR. With this two-sided approach, you’ll make the most of your data without compromising the trust your audience places in you.

Key takeaways

  • Customer data helps you personalize the experience, anticipate trends and improve your products.
  • All data collection must rest on a legal basis; for non-essential cookies and most marketing uses, that basis is consent.
  • Your users must know why, how, with whom and for how long you process their data.
  • A privacy policy, security, traceability and team training are the foundations of compliance.
  • Enforcement is very real: the CNIL issued 83 penalties in 2025.

Why collect data: understanding your customers to serve them better

Personalizing the customer experience

Personalization is key to building customer loyalty in an increasingly competitive market. Thanks to data collection, businesses can offer product recommendations, exclusive offers and promotions tailored to each customer’s preferences.

Example

An e-commerce site can use a customer’s previous purchases to recommend similar or complementary products. It increases its chances of a sale while enriching the user experience.

A word of caution, though: personalization based on personal data, especially for marketing purposes, generally requires the user’s explicit consent.

Anticipating market trends

Data collection makes it possible to anticipate trends and make informed strategic decisions. By analyzing demographic, behavioral and transactional data, you can:

  • identify new opportunities;
  • adjust your offering;
  • optimize your marketing campaigns.

A fashion company, for example, can analyze its sales to spot emerging trends and adjust its production. The result: less waste, more sales. Here again, these analyses should rely as much as possible on anonymized data, or at the very least pseudonymized data (which, for its part, is still personal data subject to the GDPR).

Continuously improving your products and services

Customer data is a valuable source of feedback. By gathering comments, reviews and suggestions, businesses identify ways to improve their products or services. An electronics manufacturer, for example, can use customer feedback to fix defects or add features.

But to do so, it is essential to keep this feedback confidential, especially if it is tied to user accounts or personal identifiers. And for this data to truly serve your projects, including your artificial intelligence projects, it has to be reliable: that’s one of the lessons of our article on why so many AI projects fail.

How to collect and use data in compliance with the GDPR

Responsible data management

Collecting, storing and processing data is a commitment: a commitment to protect it and to use it wisely. That means:

  • putting a clear privacy policy in place;
  • appointing a DPO (data protection officer), which is mandatory for public bodies and for companies whose core activities involve regular, large-scale monitoring of individuals or large-scale processing of sensitive data (Article 37 of the GDPR), and recommended in other cases;
  • carrying out a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) for processing likely to pose a high risk to individuals, as the CNIL specifies;
  • deploying strong security measures, such as encryption or segregating sensitive data.

This is the foundation for complying with the GDPR and building a climate of trust with your users.

A commitment to transparency and consent

Today, you can’t collect data without clearly telling your users:

  • why you are collecting it;
  • how you store it;
  • who you share it with;
  • and for how long.

Every processing operation must rest on one of the six legal bases set out in the GDPR (consent, contract, legal obligation, legitimate interest…). For non-essential cookies and most marketing uses, explicit consent applies: checkboxes, cookie banner, link to the privacy policy, everything must be simple, readable and easy to understand. And above all, reversible at any time. Contact forms and sign-up pages are often the entry point for your data: our advice on landing pages that convert also applies to how you obtain this consent.

The better informed users are, the more likely they are to agree with confidence.

Responsibility and accountability

As a business, you are responsible for how you use data. That includes:

  • traceability of actions;
  • the ability to respond to requests to access, modify or delete data;
  • training your teams on these issues.

Not to mention putting in place control, audit and alert mechanisms in case of a breach. The issue is anything but theoretical: according to its 2025 annual report, the CNIL issued 83 penalties totaling nearly €487 million, 21 of them relating to cookies and other trackers.

Conclusion: data, a lever to handle with care

Customer data collection is a powerful lever for improving your services, refining your marketing strategy and offering a tailored customer experience. But for it to pay off over the long term, it must be carried out in an ethical, transparent and secure way, in strict compliance with regulations such as the GDPR.

At Quiet Studio, we help you set up data collection systems that are effective AND respectful, to strengthen your users’ trust and turn it into a strategic advantage for your business. An approach in line with our commitments to responsible digital practices.

Frequently asked questions

Why should a business collect data?

To understand its customers better: personalizing their experience, anticipating market trends and improving its products and services based on their feedback.

Is consent always required to collect data?

Not always: the GDPR provides six legal bases, including contract and legitimate interest. However, consent is required for non-essential cookies and most marketing uses, and it must be possible to withdraw it at any time.

Is appointing a DPO mandatory?

Only in certain cases: public bodies, regular and large-scale monitoring of individuals, large-scale processing of sensitive data. In other cases, it is recommended.
