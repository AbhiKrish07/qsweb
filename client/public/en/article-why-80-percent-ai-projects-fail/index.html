Why 80% of AI projects fail: Quiet Studio’s guide to a successful transformation

Artificial intelligence (AI) fascinates as much as it disappoints. According to estimates cited in the 2024 RAND report, more than 80% of AI projects fail, twice the rate of conventional IT projects. And in the French Caribbean and French Guiana, businesses face additional challenges: limited infrastructure, insufficient training and resistance to change.

At Quiet Studio, we guide local businesses toward a successful AI transformation with a methodology that puts people and business objectives at the heart of every project. Here is why so many projects fail, and how to be among those that succeed.

Key takeaways

  • More than 80% of AI projects fail according to estimates cited by RAND (2024), and 95% of generative AI pilots deliver no measurable return according to MIT (2025).
  • The causes of failure are strategic and human first, not technical.
  • In the French Caribbean and French Guiana, connectivity, a shortage of experts and an economy made up of small businesses amplify the risks.
  • The Quiet Studio method in 5 steps: business audit, data, change management, targeted POC, scaling up.
  • A 15-point checklist helps you secure a project before launching it.

Beyond technology: the fatal strategic mistakes

The statistics are unforgiving. According to the Boston Consulting Group report “The Widening AI Value Gap” (September 2025, more than 1,250 companies surveyed), 60% of companies get almost no value from AI despite significant investment, and only 5% get value from it at scale. On the generative AI side, an MIT study published in 2025 estimates that 95% of corporate pilot projects deliver no measurable return.

This reality hits the French overseas territories especially hard, as they face additional constraints of their own.

The 7 most common mistakes

  1. Vague objectives: “We need to do AI, or we’ll fall behind.”
  2. Unusable data: scattered data, a common situation in French Caribbean businesses.
  3. Resistance to change: teams that are neither trained nor involved.
  4. Unsuitable infrastructure: incompatible older (legacy) systems.
  5. No governance: nobody steering the change.
  6. Underestimated budget: digital transformation projects frequently overrun their initial budget.
  7. Overly complex use cases: trying to revolutionize before optimizing.
“We need to do AI, or we’ll fall behind”: that is often the first sentence of a project doomed to fail.

French Caribbean factors that amplify the risks

In the French Caribbean and French Guiana, these universal challenges come with local constraints:

  • Connectivity: network latency and high bandwidth costs;
  • Skills: a shortage of local AI experts;
  • Ecosystem: an economy dominated by small and very small businesses;
  • Mindset: a culture of caution toward new technologies.

For a broader overview of the opportunities and challenges of generative AI in our territories, see also AWITEC’s analysis of AI in French Guiana, Martinique and Guadeloupe.

Our 5-step approach to turning failure into success

At Quiet Studio, we have developed a methodology specifically adapted to businesses in French Guiana, Martinique and Guadeloupe, which reverses the usual logic: business first, technology second.

Step 1: the business audit before the technical audit

  • Mapping existing processes
  • Identifying friction points
  • Quantifying potential gains
  • Validating organizational maturity

Step 2: assessing data maturity (data readiness assessment)

  • Data quality audit
  • Mapping available sources
  • Evaluating data governance
  • Cleanup and structuring plan

Step 3: proactive change management

  • Training teams upfront
  • Transparent communication about what’s at stake
  • Involving key stakeholders
  • A gradual upskilling plan

Step 4: a targeted, measurable POC

  • A simple but high-impact use case
  • Defined success metrics
  • A realistic timeline
  • A controlled budget

Example

Rather than trying to “automate everything” at once, we start with a simple but high-impact use case, with success metrics defined from the outset, a realistic timeline and a controlled budget. If the POC delivers, we build on what we’ve learned to gradually expand its uses.

Step 5: scaling up methodically

  • Building on lessons learned
  • Gradually expanding use cases
  • Industrializing processes
  • Continuously measuring ROI

AI, a lever for lasting transformation

Artificial intelligence is not a passing fad but a lever for lasting transformation. In the French Caribbean and French Guiana, the businesses that move past the failures of the past and adopt a methodical approach will gain a decisive head start.

The difference between the 20% who succeed and the 80% who fail? A rigorous methodology, expert support and a clear view of local issues. And to structure your processes before adding AI to them, find out how process documentation (SOPs) improves productivity.

Frequently asked questions

Why do so many AI projects fail?

Mostly for strategic and human reasons: vague objectives, unusable data, untrained teams, no governance, an underestimated budget or use cases that are too ambitious from the start.

Where should a French Caribbean business start an AI project?

With a business audit: mapping processes, identifying friction points and quantifying potential gains, before any technical question. Then come the data audit and change management.

What is a POC in artificial intelligence?

A proof of concept: a limited first project, on a simple but high-impact use case, with defined success metrics, timeline and budget. It validates the approach before scaling up.
