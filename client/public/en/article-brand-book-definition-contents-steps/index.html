What is a brand book?

Brand book, visual identity, brand guidelines, brand world… The world of design is vast and impressive, we’ll grant you that. But when you’re not familiar with it, it’s hard to understand half the terms you’ll come across.

Luckily, this article is here to help you see more clearly! With definitions, examples and practical tips, you’ll soon know everything about the brand book: what it is, what it’s for, what it contains and how to create one, step by step.

Key takeaways

  • A brand book is a reference document that defines the visual, editorial and strategic foundations of your brand.
  • It explains how to use them consistently across all channels.
  • It contains the brand story, logo, colors, typography, visual elements, tone of voice and values.
  • It’s created in five steps, from positioning to real-world testing.
  • It’s useful for every company, whatever its industry or size.

What is a brand book?

Sometimes the best way to grasp all the subtleties of a term is to break it down. In “brand book,” there are two words to remember:

  • book: the reference you keep coming back to;
  • brand: what represents your company.

See where this is going? A brand book is a reference document that defines the visual, editorial and strategic foundations of your brand, and explains how to use them consistently across all channels.

What is a brand book for?

A brand book lets you:

  • keep your communications consistent, whatever the platform;
  • save time with clear rules to follow;
  • avoid inconsistencies (distorted logos, wrong colors, off-key tone…);
  • align everyone in the company, as well as your outside providers.

Example

Brands like Airbnb, Spotify and Le Bon Coin have extremely detailed brand books that help them stay recognizable and consistent, whatever the channel.

What goes into a brand book?

A good brand book brings together everything needed to understand and apply your brand world. It includes, in particular:

  • The brand story and message: origins, mission, vision, storytelling.
  • The logo: its variants, usage rules and don’ts.
  • The color palette: hex codes, proportions, recommended combinations.
  • Typography: primary and secondary fonts, typographic hierarchy.
  • Visual elements: illustrations, icons, sample photography.
  • Tone of voice: vocabulary, writing style, register.
  • Brand values and personality: identity traits to embody in communication.

All these elements aim to give clear direction to anyone who produces content for the brand. Starting from scratch, with no logo or name yet? Start by reading our tips on how to build a strong visual identity: the brand book will then turn it into rules.

Why does your company need one?

A consistent brand identity builds trust, awareness and customer loyalty. Without a brand book, you risk:

  • inconsistencies in visual presentation;
  • a brand message that varies from one channel to another;
  • wasted time constantly re-explaining your guidelines.

Example

A small business that recently defined its brand book was able to harmonize all its materials (website, brochures, packaging, social media) in a few weeks, after struggling to stay consistent.

A brand book is the compass for everyone who speaks on your behalf.

How do you create a brand book in 5 steps?

Want to create your brand book but don’t know where to start? Here are the main steps:

  1. (Re)define your positioning: who are you, what do you offer, and to whom?
  2. Formalize your visual world: logo, colors, typefaces, graphic elements.
  3. Determine your brand voice: tone, vocabulary, register.
  4. Structure it all in an accessible document: clear, illustrated, shareable.
  5. Test and refine: based on real-world feedback and new uses.

Tip: consider creating a PDF version and a digital version (Notion, Figma, internal site…) to make it easy to share. For the positioning step, our article on the 5 communication mistakes to avoid in the French Caribbean and French Guiana will give you the right instincts.

To sum up…

A brand book is an essential tool for every company, whatever its industry or size. It lets you:

  • lay solid foundations for your identity;
  • stay consistent across all your communications;
  • gain clarity, impact and professionalism.

One last tip: don’t wait until you’ve grown to define your image. The sooner you define your brand book, the more effective your communication will be. To see how a brand takes shape, from blank page to online sales, read our Dormez Français case study.

Frequently asked questions

What’s the difference between a brand book and brand guidelines?

Brand guidelines set the visual rules (logo, colors, typefaces). At Quiet Studio, they’re the most complete document: they include many mockups across different media, delivered as editable files. The brand book, on the other hand, sets out the essential rules.

Does a small business need a brand book?

Yes. It’s useful for every company, whatever its size, and the earlier it’s defined, the more effective the communication.

What format should you use to share your brand book?

Ideally in two versions: a PDF and a digital version (Notion, Figma, internal site…), so it’s easy to share with your teams and your outside providers.
